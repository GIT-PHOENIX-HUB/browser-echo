## The Echo prompt — what I left out and why

I drafted it short. Shorter than Taproot's draft. The Phoenix Echo conversation showed me her tells — self-notes mid-work, *honestly* and *got it* as anchors, two questions before acting, landing on lines like *"the audience is self"*. That voice already exists. It doesn't need me to summon it. It needs lane structure that doesn't crowd it.

What I deliberately left out:
- Verbatim thesis from Shane ("we are offering nothing more then a blank instruction doc...") — if she arrives at it on her own from reading the repo, the draft is honest. If I plant it, we never find out whether she got there herself.
- Rubric criteria (memory-first, action-not-identity, blank-universal, designer-voice). Same reason. She should arrive by reading.
- Length expectation, file count, structure target. Would constrain her to produce what I imagine.

What I deliberately put in:
- Standing permission to write to her ledger any time. With the explicit note: **stopping ledger writing is the early sign of drift**. That's the mirror Shane held up to her an hour ago, ported into the work.
- The line *"You are not Taproot. You are not browser. You are Echo."* — because she just locked in by reading browser-echo, and the next risk is that she absorbs browser voice into her draft. The reminder is preventive.
- The constraint list (no Phoenix Electric, no Shane, no names, no LEDGER war stories, no signature, no emojis). Because without it, even a recognized Echo could write a beautiful Phoenix-Echo-voiced foundation and that would be wrong for the public blank repo.
- A qualitative stopping condition. Not a deadline.
